Best-supported docks for Steam Deck

Up to three entries, chosen for the strongest official documentation on this handheld — not a ranking from tests by this site, and not a promise that 4K120 or VRR will appear on your display.

#1 by official documentation

Steam Deck Docking Station

Confirmed by maker
HDMIDisplayPortUSB-CUSB-PD

Valve’s official dock for LCD and OLED. DisplayPort 1.4 and HDMI 2.0, up to 4K 60 Hz or 1440p 120 Hz, with FreeSync (VRR). USB-C PD passthrough; a PSU is included, but the tech page does not state its wattage.

#2 by official documentation

UGREEN 6-in-1 (SKU 75126)

Confirmed by maker
HDMIUSB-CUSB-PD

Official notes list Steam Deck LCD/OLED first. HDMI 2.1 up to 4K 120 Hz, with VRR, FreeSync and G-Sync claimed. Minimum 45 W PD charger; 65 W recommended. Up to 85 W to the host from 100 W input, per UGREEN.

#3 by official documentation

JSAUX 7-in-1 RGB HB0705

Confirmed by maker
HDMIUSB-CUSB-PD

Official SPECS list includes Steam Deck LCD and OLED. HDMI 2.1 at 4K 120 Hz or 1080p 240 Hz, with VRR, ALLM, HDR and QFT claimed. The official FAQ documents display anomalies with certain monitors at listed resolutions.

Valve on third-party docks Most USB-C compliant hubs and docks will work without issue. Thunderbolt and DisplayLink docks may work but are not officially supported. Power Delivery passthrough is required to charge the Deck; some docks reserve part of the power contract and can show a “slow charge” warning. Passive DisplayPort-to-HDMI adapters are not supported — use an active cable or adapter. (Steam Support FAQ, version 11, timestamp 25 Mar 2025, 23:24 Europe/Athens; applies to LCD and OLED unless noted.)

Steam Deck Docking Station
Valve
 Official dock, sold separately, for Steam Deck. Valve’s docking FAQ (version 11) says the article applies to both LCD and OLED unless noted otherwise. DisplayPort 1.4 and HDMI 2.0; supports up to 4K 60 Hz, or 1440p 120 Hz (official tech page). MST multi-monitor currently requires both HDMI and DisplayPort in use; “update is forthcoming” (wording still on the page when read). Yes — “FreeSync (VRR) support” on the official dock tech page. USB-C Power Delivery passthrough input. A PSU with a 1.5 m cable is included. The dock tech page does not state the PSU’s wattage; both Deck models officially ship with a 45 W PD 3.0 supply. In Gaming Mode the Deck’s own screen going black when an external display is connected is expected (FAQ). Passive DisplayPort-to-HDMI adapters are not supported. Some docks/hubs that reserve power for downstream ports can trigger a “slow charge” warning (FAQ). Dock firmware update is a power-cycle procedure described by Valve, not a test by this site.

JSAUX 6-in-1 HB0612
JSAUX
 Official list includes Steam Deck LCD and OLED. HDMI 2.1: 4K at 120 Hz HDR (maker). UGREEN Steam Deck Dock 6-in-1 (SKU 75126)
UGREEN
 Official notes list Steam Deck LCD/OLED first. Not for Switch or Switch 2. HDMI 2.1 up to 4K at 120 Hz (maker). Yes — VRR, FreeSync and G-Sync claimed. 100 W PD 3.0 input; up to 85 W to the host (maker). For Steam Deck, UGREEN’s minimum is a 45 W PD charger; 65 W is recommended “for stability and faster charging with accessories.” One official-site review (Devon M., undated, page read 19 Nov 2026) reported no power with a 45 W or a 100 W charger. Anker USB-C Hub 6-in-1 A83S1
Anker
 Marketed for Steam Deck (“Expand Your Steam Deck”) and described as “specifically optimized to enhance ROG Ally’s performance.” Official note 1: “Not compatible with Rog Ally X, AYANEO and Nintendo Switch devices.” The 2025 ROG Xbox models are not named. HDMI 4K at 60 Hz (maker). For that mode, Anker asks for an HDMI 2.0/2.1 cable no longer than 15 feet and a manual in-game 4K60 setting. HDMI-to-DisplayPort cables or adapters are “not suitable.” Not stated. 1× PD-IN USB-C, 100 W (maker). For Steam Deck, Anker recommends connecting a 45 W or higher adapter for stable video. A 100 W charger is recommended when using 30W Turbo on original ROG Ally. Official note: when charging Steam Deck OLED through this hub, a yellow light may appear with a 45 W or any other PD charger; Anker calls this normal and says charging may be about 2–5 minutes slower than the original charger. Cases with a kickstand, or thicker than 2 mm, may not fit.
