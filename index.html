Method, in brief

What goes in a cell

Every compatibility, resolution, VRR or wattage figure is tied to a named source and a retrieval date (19 Nov 2026, Europe/Athens, unless a source carries its own date). “Confirmed by maker” means the manufacturer named this device, or named it as incompatible. It is not a promise that 4K120 or VRR will appear on your television.

What stays out

No unpublished measurements, no “in our lab,” no live affiliate IDs, no tracking, no webfonts, no manufacturer product photos (no press-kit licence was obtained). Docks without a source for the device are omitted or marked Unknown. Ayn Thor, MSI Claw and OneXPlayer are not in this build.

Full sourcing rules and badge definitions.

Confirmed by maker Maker states compatibility (or incompatibility) for this device. Reported by users Dated user or support report; not a test by this site. Conflicting Official sources disagree, or one official page disagrees with itself. Unknown No clear official statement for this device, or the official page could not be read.

Naming, because it matters

ROG Ally is not one device The 2023 ROG Ally and 2024 ROG Ally X are different machines from the 2025 ROG Xbox Ally (RC73YA) and ROG Xbox Ally X (RC73XA). Several dock makers list both generations; some list only one. This site never treats “Ally X” as automatically covering “Xbox Ally X.” JSAUX’s HB0603S is the clearest example: listed for original Ally/X, and explicitly not for the 2025 Xbox models.

Official first-party docks in this build: ASUS ROG Bulwark Dock DG310, and Valve’s Steam Deck Docking Station. Lenovo’s Legion Go USB-C Dock is mentioned only as an unread official page, not as a filled-in row.
